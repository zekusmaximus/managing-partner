# Product Context: Managing Partner

## Experience

A player becomes the managing partner of a fictional Washington, D.C. government relations firm. The dashboard shows current financial and organizational health. Finances, HR, Clients, and Inbox let the player inspect details and make decisions. Advance Month applies recurring costs, collections, client and team changes, then presents new alerts and scenarios.

Staff capacity sets client service coverage: effective lobbyists and attorneys supply pooled slots, support staff improve their throughput, and overload weakens satisfaction and renewals. One pre-update coverage snapshot now sets next-month burnout pressure; ordinary month advancement does not erode efficacy. Staff recovery reduces fatigue without adding permanent skill. Players gain clients and answer client requests through the inbox. Manual overdue collections are available once per game month. The phone dashboard foregrounds cash, coverage, and alerts; phone finance reports use a selector and compact P&L period view.

Early player notes identified warnings with no obvious response and unclear action tradeoffs. Warnings now link to the relevant decision view. One major partner intervention per game month is shared by paid client recovery meetings, personally led complaint responses, and personal collection calls. Delegation uses the already-paid billable team, with a smaller satisfaction effect and no added capacity. Action previews show modeled benefits, costs, and uncertainty. The combined write-off action ends collection in the game; accounting write-off alone need not cancel a debt.

An optional guided case starts from a separate, constrained but survivable January firm. The player addresses TechTrade Association's overdue balance; reviews an opposing prospect request and may decline, hold, or pursue only a reviewed narrower monitoring scope; responds to an external policy delay and another client's complaint; and sees the anchor's April renewal or departure under existing service and satisfaction rules. Each round records a prediction and explicit decisions, including hold or defer, before advancement. Immediate and monthly feedback explains actual effects without treating external events or uncertain outcomes as proof that one choice caused them. A short April review summarizes the three decisions and asks for application to a fresh situation. The case can finish even when signing or renewal fails.

New players receive brief case orientation instead of the former 34-step tour; old tutorial progress remains legacy save history without assigning past steps to new lessons. Pausing hides guidance while keeping case progress; explicitly leaving hands the same firm to free play, and a new case still requires confirmed New Game. Contextual help and a glossary explain actual rules and label numerical targets as fictional assumptions. Progress resumes in the same browser. Receivable balances are shown by actual client where known; former-client and unassigned balances remain visible. Resolved inbox messages show the choice and its actual result. D1 freezes the exact case fixture and build. Since the likely sole user is preparing for a managing-partner role, the primary evaluation is personal immediate and delayed transfer plus ongoing decision practice; the newcomer kit remains optional. Human answers are pending, and case completion is not a mastery result.

## UX Goals

- Make the current month, cash position, and next action easy to find.
- Keep all five routes usable on phone and desktop widths.
- Make navigation, decisions, help, and tutorial actions work by keyboard.
- Give immediate, truthful feedback about the consequences of decisions.
- Keep the simplified financial model internally consistent and explain its limits honestly.

## First-Pass Boundaries

One local save slot is supported. Accounts, cloud sync, new win/loss conditions, and a full real-world accounting model are later product decisions. The game tracks an estimated tax balance and fictional late charge; its month-opening estimate does not recalculate after later actions. The optional Phase 4 newcomer kit plans 3–5 sessions, including at least two on physical phones. The primary personal protocol uses distinct baseline, immediate, and delayed questions, with first answers recorded before discussion and scores separated from usability, enjoyment, agency, and reading burden. Technical rehearsals and the earlier free-play usability script do not replace those observations; none have been collected yet.
