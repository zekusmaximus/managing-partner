The checklist below still tests the Stage 1 rules without teaching them before the participant tries them:

- In HR, ask what current coverage predicts for next-month burnout. Can they distinguish reducing fatigue through recovery from addressing continuing overload through capacity? Recovery changes burnout, not permanent efficacy.
- Compare **Lead recovery meeting** ($1,000, up to +6 satisfaction, possible overdue collection, shared attention) with **Delegate routine response** (up to +3, already-paid billable team, no extra cash or attention, no added capacity), and explicit deferral (up to −10 satisfaction). Check disabled reasons with insufficient cash, no billable staff, and spent attention.
- After a meeting or personal collection call, inspect the other route. Can they identify what used the shared allowance and when it returns? Manual collections and staff recovery retain separate limits. Explain only after the task that the allowance represents major escalations in a compressed month, not a real partner’s monthly meeting count.
- Inspect **Increase salary** and its loaded recurring cost preview. Salary cuts are outside this model; the next monthly payroll includes the increase.
- Use **Write off and close collection efforts** terminology. Ask about AR, cash, profit, and future collection in this game; separately check that the player does not infer that an accounting write-off alone always cancels a real debt.
- Ask whether reputation changes acquisition/recruitment, whether organization type alone diversifies exposure, and whether attorney/lobbyist titles change specialist capability in the game. Correct interpretations are summary-only reputation, fee/shared-issue exposure, and pooled billable capacity. Numerical benchmarks are simulation targets.

## Interim feedback (2026-09-28)

The user shared early notes without a participant count: overdue-collection and approaching-renewal warnings did not seem to lead to a useful action, and the benefits and costs of choices were unclear, especially bad-debt write-offs. A follow-up note asked for the expiring contract's value and revenue share at the warning itself, without searching the roster. These are preliminary observations, not evidence of how often the problems occur or proof that the subsequent changes work for new players. The next sessions should check whether players can read the monthly fee and current contracted-revenue share, follow warnings to collections or a client meeting, predict the modeled cash/AR/profit effects, and find the actual outcome afterward.

## Goal and participants

The following is the earlier free-play usability protocol, retained for reference. It is not the Phase 4 transfer-learning study, which still needs a frozen build, distinct pre/post questions, and participant observations.

For that earlier protocol, observe 3–5 people who have not used Managing Partner. Run individual 30–35 minute sessions; include at least two phone-width sessions. These are qualitative usability sessions, not a statistically representative balance study. Start each participant with a fresh free-play game and record the app commit and device width. Do not explain the service, collections, meeting, or pursuit rules before the tasks.

Use the same build for every participant. Before each session, choose **New Game** and confirm the reset; this clears the one browser-local save and guidance progress. If participants use separate devices, start each device with a fresh game. Share anonymized notes from the table below after the sessions for analysis and the next iteration.

## Facilitator script

1. **First impression (3 minutes).** Ask: “What is your role, what is the firm’s current position, and what would you inspect before advancing?” Note which dashboard metrics and alerts they notice first.
2. **Service and retention (8 minutes).** Ask them to inspect HR and Clients, describe how staffing might affect service and renewals, make one staffing choice, then advance several months. When a contract or satisfaction warning appears, ask what they would do and let them find the client meeting without pointing it out. For an expiry warning, ask how much monthly revenue would be lost if that client left, what share of the current contracted monthly fees it represents, and how they found those figures. Ask which actions share the monthly partner intervention and whether this meeting guarantees renewal. Before each advance ask what they expect to happen.
3. **Cash decisions (6 minutes).** Ask them to find overdue receivables and use the manual collections action. When an overdue warning appears, ask them to follow it to a response and compare the collection attempt, a client meeting, and a write-off. Before a write-off, ask what would happen to AR, cash, profit, and the chance to collect that balance later. Ask whether they can repeat either monthly action and where they learned the limits. After acting, ask whether the warning still describes the current situation, how they would check, and what they would do with a resolved or historical alert.
4. **Inbox decision (6 minutes).** Ask them to handle a pending client or business opportunity if one appears, then find the recorded outcome. If a new-client opportunity appears, ask them to compare aggressive pursuit, initial contact, and passing: the signing chance and up-front cost of each, whether any cost depends on signing, and how a signed client could change service coverage. Ask what they expect to happen to cash and profit before they choose. If no opportunity appears, record that and continue rather than manufacturing one.
5. **Phone finances (4 minutes, phone sessions).** Ask them to find Accounts Receivable and the P&L year-to-date profit without pointing to the tabs or table scroll area.
6. **Wrap-up (3 minutes).** Ask: “What drove client satisfaction and renewals? Which choice felt least fair or least clear? What would you do next month?”

The facilitator may pause or stop if the participant is stuck, but should record the point and prompt given. Avoid teaching the interface during a task.

Group observations by repeated problems, then prioritize any issue that blocks a decision or causes players to form a false causal model. Check whether participants understand that staffing affects service and renewal, can follow and resolve a warning, can distinguish collections from write-offs, can explain the cost and benefit of a meeting or new-client pursuit, and can reach relevant mobile finance data. Re-run the seeded balance report after rule changes, but keep those scripted results separate from participant observations.
