# Managing Partner: personal learning protocol

**Status (September 30, 2026): ready to use; personal sessions and answers pending.** The intended learner expects to become managing partner of their firm in roughly 3–5 years and may be the game's only user. The primary question is whether the game helps that person reason through partner decisions and retain that reasoning, not whether a small newcomer sample enjoys the interface. The [D1 newcomer kit](learning-study-kit.md) remains available for optional usability sessions; its recruitment target is not a prerequisite for this plan.

## What is fixed, and what is measured

Use the exact guided-case code and January fixture at tag `learning-study-d1` (commit `ac6738422382151686b7a0f9ad47f497e117a3f4`). Its opening firm has eight clients, five employees, $45,000 cash, $33,000 accounts receivable, $16,589 reported profit, and an April anchor renewal. Record the tag and commit for the first play. A future gameplay revision begins a new version of the record; do not silently compare it as if the teaching material were identical. The tag pins the build, not a learner's browser save. Use a separate browser profile or origin for the measured case and leave an existing personal save untouched. If the learner has already played this case or read its answer key, record that exposure; the first score is then a current-state baseline, not a first-exposure baseline.

Use the existing [pretest](learning-study-pretest.md) and distinct [posttest](learning-study-posttest.md) as short, written transfer probes. Their firms, amounts, and situations differ from the game's April application question. Use the [0–2 causal-reasoning rubric](learning-study-kit.md#02-causal-reasoning-rubric) for each of four concepts: **C** cash versus profit, **G** growth/conflicts/capacity, **A** attention/delegation, and **U** policy/renewal uncertainty. Score the *first* unassisted answer, with the reasoning, tradeoff, and missing facts visible. `NA` means a question was skipped or interrupted, not a zero. Keep the rubric and example answers out of view until both immediate forms are complete.

A repeated scripted case is practice, not a new independent posttest. |

For the measured run, use the same browser/build/fixture throughout. There is no need to erase the main personal save. A physical-phone self-check can reveal navigation or reading problems, but it is a usability check and does not substitute for delayed reasoning. If a mentor or experienced managing partner can review the **de-identified** answers, ask them to challenge omitted considerations after the first answers are locked; log that feedback and do not revise the original score.

## Delayed transfer form: use only at the delayed check

These are different firms, names, and numbers from the game and the immediate forms. Answer each in your own words before viewing the rubric or earlier answers. An honest “I don't know” is useful.

**C · Cash and profit.** Calder Public Affairs reports $31,200 of August profit, holds $5,900 cash, and has a $16,700 invoice for work billed in July. A $7,200 tax payment and $11,400 payroll are due soon. The invoice clears today. What changes now in cash, receivables, and August profit? What would you check before a partner distribution?

**G · Growth and fit.** Meridian Counsel has six clients and little spare billable capacity. A transit supplier offers $12,600 a month for work on a procurement rule; an existing rider coalition opposes the supplier's position. A hire would add $6,100 in initial cost and recurring pay. What must be reviewed before accepting, and how could the partner compare a narrower scope, a delay, and declining?

**A · Partner attention.** Elm Street Policy has one senior-partner escalation slot this period. An existing client disputes a deliverable, a trained associate can prepare a routine legislative update, and a prospect needs a possible-conflict review. What should the partner own, delegate, or oversee? What is given up if the partner personally leads the client conversation?

**U · Uncertain result.** Northbridge Civic completed its agreed briefings, but a commission postpones its hearing by six weeks. A client later questions renewal despite positive service reports. What can the partner infer, what further evidence is needed, and what should be communicated? Would another paid meeting guarantee a policy or renewal result?

Keep raw answers private and outside the repository; only de-identified summaries belong in project notes. Copy a row for each concept and occasion. Score after the first answers are locked. Keep game completion, usability, enjoyment, agency, and reading burden in separate columns or notes.

For each concept, compare **baseline → immediate → delayed** first-answer scores and quote the exact reasoning that improved, persisted, or weakened. A starting score of 2 is prior competence or a ceiling on this short probe; look for a harder, fresh decision and better calibration rather than calling it “no learning.” A higher immediate score followed by a lower delayed score is a retention gap. A high-confidence guarantee that money buys a policy win or renewal is a serious misconception even if other parts of the answer are sound. Use the same 0–2 rules across forms, but do not turn four scores into a claim that the game caused professional readiness.

At each quarterly review, list one decision the game clarified, one real-world factor its simplified model omits, and one concrete practice to try or question to ask an experienced partner. Compare your confidence with outcomes and document alternative explanations; external policy movement and client renewal have causes beyond the firm's actions. Real firm decisions still require actual financial records, conflict procedures, client terms, and professional advice where appropriate. The game's fictional targets and one-attention-slot rule are teaching devices, not operating rules for your firm.

**Current evidence:** the build, tests, browser rehearsal, and scripted balance runs are complete. No personal baseline, immediate, delayed, or longitudinal answers have been collected. No learning or readiness conclusion is supported yet.
